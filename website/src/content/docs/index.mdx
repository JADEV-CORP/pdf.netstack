  - q: Is PdfNetStack free for commercial use?
    a: Yes. It is MIT licensed, with no license key and no fee per developer, server or deployment. The OCR models package adds Apache-2.0, which is free for commercial use too.
  - q: Does compressing a PDF with PdfNetStack change how it looks?
    a: Not by default. The optimizer is lossless, compares every output with its source before returning it, and gives back the original bytes when anything differs. Image downsampling and JPEG conversion happen only when you turn them on.
  - q: Does it need Ghostscript, a browser or native libraries?
    a: No. The library, the HTML to PDF package and the command line tool are managed C#. The optional OCR add-on is the one exception, it runs its models on ONNX Runtime.
  - q: Which .NET versions does it support?
    a: .NET 8, 9, 10 and later, with trimming and Native AOT, and .NET Framework 4.6.2 to 4.8.1, Mono and Unity through netstandard2.0.
  - q: Can PdfNetStack replace PDFsharp?
    a: For drawing, editing, merging, splitting, forms and encryption, PdfNetStack has MIT licensed equivalents, and the migration guide maps the common calls and lists the gaps. MigraDoc's document model has no equivalent; for documents laid out from content, PdfNetStack.Html converts HTML and CSS to PDF.
  - q: Is PdfNetStack related to the PDFNet SDK of Apryse?
    a: No. PDFNet is a commercial SDK from Apryse. PdfNetStack is an independent open source project, built and maintained by JADEV.

## How compression works

<Steps>

1. **Read.** The file is parsed with limits on nesting, stream sizes and image sizes. Encrypted,
   signed and damaged files stop here and come back unchanged, with the reason.

2. **Rewrite.** Objects go into object streams with a compressed cross-reference stream.
   Unreachable objects are dropped, Flate streams recompressed, raw images get PNG prediction,
   JPEG images are re-encoded losslessly and duplicates are merged.

3. **Prove.** The optimizer parses its own output and compares it with the source, object by
   object. Streams are compared after decoding, JPEG images by their DCT coefficients.

4. **Return.** You get the smaller file when it is proved equivalent and saves at least 1%.
   Otherwise you get your original bytes, and `result.Reason` tells you why.

</Steps>

Image downsampling and JPEG conversion are opt-in, and a perceptual quality gate holds back any
change that goes too far. [Compress PDF](/guides/compress-pdf/)

## Performance

### Public corpus, against qpdf and Ghostscript

CI downloads 77 openly licensed and public domain PDFs (NIST and NASA publications, arXiv papers,
government gazettes, test files) and runs PdfNetStack with its defaults next to qpdf, MuPDF and
Ghostscript. Each output is scored on size, on rendering in PDFium and on what it kept: tags,
forms, outlines, signatures, usage rights and PDF/A conformance. On the 76 files PDFium can open,
18 November 2026:

| Tool | Saved | Outputs that keep quality |
|---|---:|---:|
| PdfNetStack, lossless (defaults) | 11.2% | 76 of 76 |
| qpdf 12.4.2, lossless | 5.7% | 64 of 76 |
| PdfNetStack, balanced preset | 21.8% | 76 of 76 |
| Ghostscript 10.05, `/ebook` | 32.1% | 23 of 76 |

On the 64 files where both keep quality, PdfNetStack's output is 8% smaller than qpdf's, in about
the same time on one thread, its proof of every output included. Signed and encrypted files are
kept as they are, by design. Method and how to run it yourself on [Benchmarks](/benchmarks/).

### Private corpus

  <div><strong>1,296</strong> <span>invoices, reports, manuals, scans and slides, 569 MiB</span></div>
